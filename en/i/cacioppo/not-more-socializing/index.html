More socialising doesn't fix it

The meta-analysis ranks it third

Masi, Chen, Hawkley & Cacioppo, meta-analysis of loneliness interventions, 2011

The most common advice for loneliness performs worst. What came first in the data.

John CacioppoThe meta-analysis ranks it third

What actually happened?

In 2011 Cacioppo and colleagues pooled the loneliness interventions into four kinds: more chances to socialise, more social support, better social skills, and correcting how social situations get read. The first three are everyone's instinct, and none of them did much. The fourth came first — going after automatic conclusions like 'they must find me boring' and 'if I reach out I'll be a nuisance.' The reason is the same as the last piece: the stuck layer is the reading.

When opportunity isn't scarce, adding opportunity does nothing

A university campus, a big city, a company offsite — the objective chances to socialise are plentiful, and lonely people are lonely in all of them. When the bottleneck isn't opportunity, more of it just delivers 'none of these people are mine' more often.

Ten events and you still went home alone. That usually isn't a shortage of events.

Skills rank third, because most people aren't short of them

Filing loneliness under 'bad at talking to people' is the other wrong turn. Skills training came third in the data, because most lonely people are fine everywhere else — they can do it, they just answer on the other person's behalf at the critical moment.

Easy company all day at work, and the message saying 'lunch this weekend?' never gets sent.

What changes is the sentence that appears on its own

The move is small: catch the automatic conclusion, write it down, ask what it rests on. 'He didn't reply' is a fact. 'He doesn't want to talk to me' is a conclusion. You added the step in between. Separate them, then look for another reading.

Rewrite 'I'm annoying him' as 'he might be busy', and send one more message tomorrow.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you did what everyone said, went to the things, met people, and you're still lonely.

Ask first: the last time I didn't ask someone, was it for lack of a chance, or because I answered for them first? If it's the second, adding events is a road to nowhere.

Where it goes wrong: dropping social occasions altogether — opportunity isn't the most effective factor but it is still necessary; and this is not 'it's all in your head.' Some relationships genuinely aren't worth keeping.
