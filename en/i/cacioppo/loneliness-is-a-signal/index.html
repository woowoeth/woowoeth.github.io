Loneliness is a kind of hunger

A signal, not a personality

Cacioppo & Patrick, Loneliness, 2008

What loneliness actually is. Why thirty years of research put it in the same category as hunger and thirst.

John CacioppoA signal, not a personality

What actually happened?

Cacioppo spent thirty years on loneliness, and the first thing he did was tighten the definition: loneliness is not being alone. It's the gap between the connection you want and the connection you have. Which is why a crowded table can be lonely and living alone need not be. Tighten the definition and the category changes too — he filed it with hunger, thirst and pain. All of them are the body flagging something you need to survive.

Call it a defect and you hide it, which closes the only exit

Read loneliness as a flaw in your character and the first instinct is to say nothing — which seals the one way out. As a signal it carries no shame: it doesn't say something is wrong with you, it says something needs doing.

'I'm just not a people person.' The moment that sentence is out, every available move is switched off.

Alone and lonely are different, so most advice aims wrong

With the definition tightened, a lot of standard advice stops working. Move to a bigger city, go to more things — all of it is the opposite of solitude, and none of it closes the gap. The test is subjective: does anyone here know you.

Dinner plans every night and emptier each time — the thing being added isn't on the ledger.

It has physical consequences, not just unpleasant ones

Chronically lonely people show higher blood pressure, worse inflammatory markers, lighter sleep and faster cognitive decline. Cacioppo pressed this point to move the subject out of the 'being precious' box. The body is settling the bill.

Across multiple studies the effect of long-term loneliness on mortality lands in the same range as long-term smoking.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you feel lonely, and feel embarrassed to say so.

Ask first: am I missing people being present, or people knowing me? The two answers lead to completely different moves — the first is helped by crowds, the second is not.

Where it goes wrong: hearing it as an order to widen your circle immediately, which only adds volume; or turning it back on yourself for not being outgoing enough. The signal doesn't check your personality first — extroverts get it too.

Lines to keep

Hungry, you go find food. This is the same kind of reminder.

People near you, and people who know you, are not the same count.
