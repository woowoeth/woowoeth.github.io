Give yourself time with no scoreboard

Any standard becomes a thing to optimise

How to find out what you actually want. The condition that has to be true first.

What actually happened?

Deresiewicz's prescription isn't a new major or a new job. It's a stretch of time with no evaluation attached — doing one thing that isn't scored, for long enough. The reasoning is direct: only when nobody is grading you do you find out what you actually want. Where a standard exists, people optimise it automatically, and the optimising drowns out the signal about whether you want to do this at all. The problem isn't that you can't think it through. It's that no environment lets the question surface.

A score existing means you'll chase it

This isn't weak will, it's the default. Wherever there's a comparable metric, attention slides to it. So answering 'what do I want' requires temporarily leaving the places that have metrics.

A hobby starts taking commissions and counting followers, and quickly becomes a second job. Not corruption — a score.

It only works if it's long enough

A few days tests novelty. The signal that counts is whether you still want to continue after the novelty has gone and it's got a bit boring. He's describing months, not a couple of weekends.

Six months on something nobody is watching produces better judgement than any careers course.

Separate 'I want this' from 'this is the next hurdle'

This is the usable daily test. Facing an opportunity, ask whether what draws you is the content itself or the fact that it's the obvious next step on the current track. The second disguises itself very well as the first.

A promotion on the table: separate wanting the work from not wanting to look like you've stalled.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you're making a choice about direction.

Ask first: is it the content that draws me, or is it simply the next hurdle? Have I done anything unscored lately?

Where it goes wrong: reading it as a reason to quit and wander. He's describing carving out an unscored space, not demolishing the structure.

Lines to keep

Only when nobody is grading you do you find out what you want.

Wherever a comparable metric exists, attention slides to it.

Still willing after the novelty is gone — that's the signal.
