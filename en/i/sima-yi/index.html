Patience

Sima Yi (Three Kingdoms · 179–251) — Patience.

Strategy and competitionQin, Han and the Six DynastiesPatience
He outlasted four rulers of Wei and at seventy took control of the state in a single morning. The record has two operations in it that look opposite and are not: a march that arrived in eight days when a month was expected, and ten years of illness that were not illness. Both work off the same insight, which is that the other side's plan is built out of assumptions about what you will do.

What actually happened?

The standoff at the Wuzhang Plains. Zhuge Liang sent him a set of women's clothes, a public insult meant to shame him into giving battle. He did not lose his temper. He accepted the gift pleasantly, and asked the messenger how the chancellor had been sleeping. The messenger said his master rose early, stayed late and ate very little. Sima Yi turned away and said that Liang was not going to live long. Zhuge Liang died that year, at fifty-four. Sima Yi lived another seventeen.

Their timetable is a list of your openings

Meng Da worked out precisely how long Sima Yi would need: the distance, plus a month for a memorial to the throne and a reply. Every figure was right. The one error was assuming the procedure would be followed. Sima Yi did not report, and was under the walls in eight days.

They have always shipped at quarter end, so every defence is scheduled for quarter end. The real threat comes when they skip the process.

Feign weakness until the watching stops

Half-hearted quietness leaves the eyes on you. When Cao Shuang sent a man to look him over, he could not hold the cup and let the porridge run down his chest, and mistook one province for another twice. The report came back that he was a breathing corpse. Then the surveillance went.

To move a rival's attention off you, vague public positioning is not enough. There has to be nothing worth following up.

The window is recognised, not made

He waited on one specific condition: the Cao brothers out of the capital on the same day, the centre of power and the centre of force both absent at once. He did not create that. The whole function of the patience was to still be there, intact, when it came.

Turning points in an industry are usually triggered outside your control. What you control is being at the table that day with cards in hand.

Defence as the cheaper form of attack

At the Wuzhang Plains he declined battle for months while the other side burned supply and, as it turned out, a life. His logic was plain: I do not need to win, I need not to lose, and the clock does the rest. He read life expectancy where others read order of battle.

With fewer resources than a rival, stop trying to settle it in one engagement. Make each contact cost them more than it costs you.

How do I use it today?

If you are waiting, write down the exact condition you are waiting for, in a sentence someone else could verify. If you cannot write it, you are not waiting, you are stalling. Then ask the second question: on the day it appears, could you finish the whole thing at once?
