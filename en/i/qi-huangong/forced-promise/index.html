Duke Huan of Qi

A promise made at knife point

Whether you keep your word is something everyone watching remembers

Sima Qian, Records of the Grand Historian, Hereditary House of Taigong of Qi

He agreed with a dagger at his throat. When a promise is forced out of you, do you still keep it afterwards?

Duke Huan of QiWhether you keep your word is something everyone watching remembers

What actually happened?

In the duke's fifth year Qi defeated Lu, and the two states met at Ke to swear peace. On the altar the Lu general Cao Mo suddenly seized the duke with a dagger and demanded the land Qi had taken. The duke agreed. Cao Mo put the dagger down and went back to his place. Then the duke had second thoughts: keep the land, kill Cao Mo. Guan Zhong told him that breaking faith and killing the man would buy one small moment of satisfaction and cost him the trust of every state. The duke handed back all of it.

The account is not with him alone

Guan Zhong's arithmetic was cold. Killing Cao Mo would earn one small satisfaction. Breaking faith would cost the trust of every lord. How unreasonable the man who forced you was is between you and him; whether your word holds is what everyone watching remembers.

A client corners you in a group chat and you agree to a refund. Change the process afterwards, drop the client, but pay this one.

Land given back, a hegemon made

The record goes on: when the lords heard, they all trusted Qi and wanted to join it. Two years later they gathered to meet the duke at Juan, and from then on he led them. The land was a real loss; it became the capital of his leadership.

The deal where you gave ground under pressure tells others that your word holds. The next big client may come from that.

Regret is allowed; it does not get to decide

Sima Qian does not cover for him: the duke regretted it and wanted the man dead. What he got right was a single thing. He stopped before acting and let someone who was not angry finish the sums.

The night you want to withdraw a promise, send nothing. Tell someone who was not there what keeping it and breaking it would each cost.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: someone pushed you into agreeing to something in front of others, and the longer you think about it the more you want a reason to back out.

Ask first: who saw this? If I renege I get to vent; whose trust do I lose?

Where it goes wrong: reading it as 'every forced promise must be kept'. Guan Zhong's reason was not that a pledge is sacred. It was that every state was watching and Qi needed their support. Something signed under fraud or a threat, something that should never have been signed, is not covered. Before deciding, be clear whose trust you are after.
