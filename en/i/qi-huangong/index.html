Duke Huan of Qi

Keeping faith

Duke Huan of Qi (Spring and Autumn · Qi · died 643 BC) — Keeping faith.

Power and organisationPre-Qin and classicalKeeping faith
Duke Huan of Qi, personal name Xiaobai, ruled the state of Qi for forty-three years from 685 BC. He made Guan Zhong, the man who had once shot an arrow at him, his chief minister, and became the first hegemon of the Spring and Autumn period. Everything here follows Sima Qian. He is here for two situations: whether a promise forced out of you still binds, and what to make of someone who is too devoted. He got the first right and the second wrong.

What actually happened?

Qi was in turmoil, its rulers killed one after another, and two exiled princes raced home to claim it. Lu sent Guan Zhong with troops to block the road, and his arrow struck Prince Xiaobai on the belt hook. Xiaobai played dead. Guan Zhong sent word to Lu, and the party escorting the rival prince slowed down; by the time it arrived six days later, Xiaobai was already in the capital and on the throne. He then demanded that Lu hand Guan Zhong over so he could have him chopped to pieces. What he actually meant to do was employ him.

One sentence changed his mind

Bao Shuya told him: to govern Qi, Gao Xi and I are enough; to lead the realm, you need Guan Zhong. The duke let go of the arrow, made Guan Zhong a high officer and handed him the government.

The person you most want to punish may be the one the job most needs. Decide which you are after.

When they say you need not bow

At the gathering at Kuiqiu the Zhou king sent sacrificial meat and said the duke need not bow. He was ready to accept it so; Guan Zhong said no, and he bowed. Later that year, Sima Qian says, he grew prouder, and lords began to drift away.

When a superior says there is no need for formality between you, keep the formality anyway.

The moment he wanted a ceremony

He counted his achievements, nine gatherings of the lords and the realm set right, and wanted to perform the great sacrifice at Mount Tai. Guan Zhong argued and failed, then said the rite needed rare tribute from distant lands first. Only then did the duke drop it.

The moment you most want to celebrate yourself is when you most need someone willing to stop you.

How do I use it today?

This week list the three people around you who agree with you most. Beside each, write when they last told you something would not work. For the one you cannot remember, take them a decision you are unsure about next week and see whether they dare say no.

Read alongside

Duke Wen of JinBoth were hegemons, and both employed men who had tried to harm them: Guan Zhong's arrow hit Xiaobai's belt hook, the eunuch Pi cut off Chong'er's sleeve. They part on listening. Chong'er kept listening to the people who pushed back. Duke Huan listened to Guan Zhong for forty years and ignored him the one time it mattered most, keeping only the men who pleased him.
