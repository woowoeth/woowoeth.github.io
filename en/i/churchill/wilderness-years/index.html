The wilderness years

The decade nobody called him

Churchill out of office, 1929–1939

Shut out, nobody consulting you, and no end in sight. What Churchill did with each of those days.

Winston ChurchillThe decade nobody called him

What actually happened?

In 1929 he went out with the government, lost the Exchequer, and held no office for the next ten years. He was not idle. He wrote for a living — the four-volume life of Marlborough and the History of the English-Speaking Peoples both came out of that decade, and the fees kept the household. At Chartwell he laid brick himself, building walls and a cottage, and had joined a bricklayers' union. When there was time left he painted. Ten years without a title, and something left his hands every day.

When nobody calls you, make things that leave your hands

The hard part of being shut out isn't the idleness, it's not knowing whether you still count. His method was to make every day deliver: a chapter, a stretch of wall, a painting. Someone with output doesn't need an invitation to confirm they're still here.

For this stretch, fix on one thing you can finish daily, however small the finished thing is.

The bricklaying wasn't a hobby. It put him back in his body

He built real walls, not photographs of walls. Writing spends the head; laying brick spends the arms. Use both in a day and you don't spend all of it arguing in your head with people who aren't there — which is the trap of being sidelined. Sweat pulls you out.

Put one physically hard task in the day and let it compete with the trial running in your head.

It fed the family, which is why it wasn't waiting

The fees were his living for that decade; writing wasn't a pastime, it was the profession. The distinction matters: once something actually supports you, you treat it differently, and it stops being an interval before going back.

Treat what you do in this stretch as a job — someone paying, a deadline. That's what makes it hold you.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you've been pushed out of the centre, nobody asks your view, and you don't know how long this lasts.

Ask first: what did I deliver today? Does anyone pay for it, does it have a deadline? Have I arranged something physical?

Where it goes wrong: reading it as 'endure and you'll be back' — he spent no day enduring, he produced, and he might well never have gone back; and don't romanticise the wall. It was hard labour he set himself, not a pose.
