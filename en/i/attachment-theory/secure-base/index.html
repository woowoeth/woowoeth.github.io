A secure base

You go far only if you can come back

Bowlby, Attachment and Loss; A Secure Base

Why some people will take a risk and others will not. The condition that has to be in place before nerve is available.

What actually happened?

Bowlby worked with children who had lost their families in the war, and argued that the pull towards closeness is an instinct rather than a dependency to be outgrown. His most productive idea is the secure base: a child explores only while sure the mother is still where she was, and the moment that is in doubt exploration stops and all attention comes back to the relationship. He insisted this is not a nursery phenomenon. Adults run the same circuit, with a partner, a friend or a steady institution standing in the base's place.

Support is not doing it for them; it is that the base is still there

A secure base does not supply help, it supplies certainty. Which is why the sentence that works is not I will do it for you but if this goes wrong we clear it up together. It removes the surcharge on failure, and the surcharge is usually what people are actually afraid of.

If you want somebody to decide, say plainly that a wrong call will not land on them personally. It moves more than any incentive does.

Uncertainty costs more than bad news

In Bowlby's observations the children in unstable relationships explored least — not because they had been hurt but because they had to keep monitoring. A clear bad outcome can at least be adapted to. An unresolved one keeps taking bandwidth for as long as it hangs there.

Announcing a reorganisation late usually costs more output than the reorganisation itself does.

The base has to be reliable, not perfect

Caregivers of secure children get it wrong, lose patience, misread things. What separates them is that they are predictable overall and they repair afterwards. For anyone raising a child or running a team that is the load off: not right every time, but steady, and mended when it breaks.

You shouted, and afterwards said plainly that you should not have. The repair is worth more than the loss of temper cost.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you want somebody to carry more than they currently will.

Ask first: what does failure cost them here, and am I supplying help or supplying certainty?

Where it goes wrong: turning the base into cover for everything, so that nobody ever meets a consequence — that is not a base, it is a subsidy.

Lines to keep

We are happiest when life is a series of excursions from a secure base.

The need for closeness is instinct, not a dependency to be outgrown.

An uncertain relationship costs more than a clearly distant one.
