Shun (Legendary age · Records of the Grand Historian) — Family.

Family and relationshipsPre-Qin and classicalFamily
Shun, one of the legendary Five Emperors. Yao passed the realm to him and he passed it to Yu, and 'Yao and Shun' became the Chinese byword for good rulers. He was born a commoner; his father, his stepmother and his half-brother Xiang all tried to kill him, and by twenty he was known for devotion to them. Everything here follows Sima Qian and Mencius. He is here for families that do real harm, where neither giving in nor cutting off will do.

What actually happened?

When Yao grew old he asked who could be trusted, and the chiefs of the four quarters named Shun. Yao gave him his two daughters in marriage, to see how he ran his house, and sent his nine sons to live beside him, to see how he dealt with others. Where Shun farmed, at Mount Li, people yielded field boundaries to one another. Where he fished, they yielded the best spots. Where he made pots, no pot was shoddy. Wherever he settled, in a year it was a village, in two a town, in three a city.

A thirty-one-year trial

Yao raised him at thirty, had him act for the throne at fifty, and died when Shun was fifty-eight. Shun took the throne at sixty-one. For thirty-one years he was watched and tested before he was given the thing itself.

The long probation before anyone trusts you is part of the answer, not a delay before it.

The imperial banner at his father's door

As emperor he went to pay his respects to his father under the imperial banner, with all the care of a son. The ranks between them had turned over; what he did there was still a son's part.

Once you have the upper hand, you need not go back to collect an apology.

Not to his own son

His son Shangjun was unworthy, so Shun put Yu forward as his successor. After Shun died and the mourning ended, Yu stepped aside for Shangjun, but the lords all went to Yu, and only then did he take the throne.

Give the post to the most capable person, not the closest one.

How do I use it today?

This week think of the person at home who is hardest on you. Make two columns: what I can give him (time, money, company) and what I will not put in his hands (keys, passwords, decisions about my life). Look at the page before you next see him.

Read alongside

The Duke of ZhouBoth had brothers who turned on them. Xiang tried to kill Shun, and Shun as emperor gave him a fief but no say in running it. The Duke of Zhou's brothers Guan and Cai joined Wugeng's rising, and the Duke marched against them, executing Guan and exiling Cai. Xiang threatened one man; Guan took up arms against the realm. Once others are hurt, kinship no longer covers it.
