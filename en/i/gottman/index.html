Four fatal signals

John Gottman (1994 · University of Washington) — Four fatal signals.

Family and relationshipsModernFour fatal signals

What did this one leave behind?

A psychologist who recorded thousands of couples in a laboratory, coding expression, tone and heart rate frame by frame, then tracked who divorced. His conclusion doesn't rest on theory: from fifteen minutes of one argument he classified couples with reported accuracy above ninety per cent. What predicts a breakup is not how fiercely people fight but whether four specific signals appear while they do.

What actually happened?

The ninety per cent needs a caveat, and it is the honest part of this entry. Those figures come largely from models fitted to the same data they were then measured on, which inflates accuracy; independent prospective tests have come out lower. What survives the criticism is the direction, not the number: contempt, criticism, defensiveness and stonewalling really do predict worse outcomes, and contempt really is the strongest of the four. Treat it as a set of things to watch for in yourself, not as an instrument.

The parts · Criticism and complaint are different things

A complaint targets behaviour and can be solved. Criticism targets character and can only be defended. Many people think they're raising an issue while every sentence defines the other person — which is how one problem runs for ten years.

The parts · Contempt is the strongest single signal

Eye-rolling, mockery, mimicking their tone. It differs from anger: anger is about the event, contempt says the person is unworthy. It destroys the foundation rather than one conversation.

The parts · Repair succeeds on the ordinary days

Happy couples aren't the ones who don't fight; they're the ones who reach for the brake and have it taken. Whether it gets taken depends on the balance built up beforehand — roughly five positive interactions to one negative in stable relationships.

How do I use it today?

When the same thing gets fought about and every round escalates, check the last sentence you said: was it about the event or about the person? Any sarcasm? Were you explaining or returning the ball? And notice whether they offered a way out that you didn't take — taking it is harder than offering it, and it is where most of this stalls.

Lines to keep

The strongest single predictor of divorce is contempt.

Criticism targets the person. A complaint targets the act.

Happy couples aren't the ones who don't fight. They repair.
