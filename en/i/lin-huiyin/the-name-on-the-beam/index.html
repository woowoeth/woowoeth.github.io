What actually happened?

In June 1937 Liang Sicheng, Lin Huiyin, Mo Zongjiang and a technician rode mules into the Wutai mountains and found the great hall of Foguang Temple. Ink lettering ran under the beams, hidden by red paint, two zhang above the floor in poor light; the team could read only a few official titles. Lin, long-sighted, alone made out the name of the woman disciple Ning Gongyu. Fearing a mistake, she checked the stone pillar outside: the same name, as patron of the hall, dated 857.

Treat what only you see as a hypothesis

Only she could read the name. She did not announce it. Her first thought was that she might be wrong, so she looked for the same name on the pillar outside. Written on the beam, carved on the pillar: only when the two matched did the date stand.

Finish the clumsy method

To read the whole inscription they sent a monk to the village for workers; he found two old farmers, and building a scaffold took another day. They tore bedsheets, soaked them, and passed them up to wash off the paint. Wet, the ink appeared; dry, it faded. It took three days.

An old archive is too faded to scan. Read it page by page under a lamp, copy it out, then check.

The third witness was sitting in the hall

Beside a guardian king on the altar sat a life-size figure of a laywoman. Against the names on beam and pillar, they recognised her as Ning Gongyu, who paid for the hall. Inscription, pillar and statue agreed, and a Tang hall was dated.

Looking into an incident, do not just ask the people involved; check the messages and photos from the time, and conclude when three agree.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: something important that only you have noticed, and you are not sure yourself.

Ask first: can I find the same thing somewhere else? Is there a third piece of evidence? What is the slow, clumsy way to read all of it?

Where it goes wrong: letting the fear of being wrong become silence. She feared a mistake, so she went to the pillar; once it matched, she said so.
