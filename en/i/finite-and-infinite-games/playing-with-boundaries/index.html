The two kinds of player treat rules in opposite ways. What separates changing the rules from simply cheating.

Finite and Infinite GamesRules are props, not walls

What actually happened?

Carse sets the two players against each other. The rules of a finite game are fixed before play and may not change, because their whole job is to let the game produce a winner. The rules of an infinite game must change during play, and the only test of a change is whether more people can keep playing. Finite players treat rules as walls and strain inside them; infinite players treat them as props. Whoever must win is the one the rules will trap. Finite players train to be powerful; infinite players stay flexible.

The right to change a rule comes from wanting play to continue

Changing rules and cheating look identical from outside. The difference is who benefits: change it so you win this round and it is cheating; change it so the play can continue and it is the infinite player's job. Ask whether the change widened the field or narrowed it.

A platform reworks its revenue share. Done for this quarter's margin it is killing the game; done to keep small creators alive it is extending the game. The action can look the same.

The sign the rules own you is that you only win by them

The longer you win inside one set of rules, the more your ability grows into the shape of those rules. Rankings, reviews and algorithms change, and years of accumulation reset. The defence is a standing question: if these rules were replaced tomorrow, what would I still have?

Skills tuned entirely to one platform's algorithm go to zero the week it updates. What travels is craft, reputation, and the people who follow you.

Power is for what you have seen, flexibility for what you have not

Training to be strong prepares you for matches already played. Staying flexible admits that the match that matters has not happened yet. The two need different reserves: one is doing a single thing perfectly, the other is keeping the slack that efficiency would have sold.

An organisation optimised for today's business, every person fully loaded and no slack anywhere, is unbeatable until the rules change.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: the rules you work inside are turning against you.

Ask first: am I trying to win the next round under these rules, or should I be in the room where they get written? Would my change let more people play, or only me?

Where it goes wrong: using the rules should change as a sore loser's excuse, or staying the perfect student of a system until you are shaped like it.
