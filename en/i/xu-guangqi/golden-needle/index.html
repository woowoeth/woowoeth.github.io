What actually happened?

In the spring of 1607 the first six books of the Elements were finished. An old Chinese saying went: show people the embroidered mandarin ducks, but never hand over the golden needle. In his notes on the book Xu turned it round: hand over the needle for others to use, and never mind the finished ducks. This book, he added, does more than give you a needle. It teaches you to mine the ore, smelt the iron and draw the wire to make one.

Results are the ducks, reasoning is the needle

The European calendar methods gave accurate results. That was the finished embroidery. Xu wanted the reasoning that let him work them out again himself. A borrowed result fails when conditions change; with the needle you can stitch a new pattern.

A formula copied from somewhere breaks in a new setting. One you have derived, you can adapt.

What can be derived need not be guessed

He wrote that this book left nothing to doubt, guess, test or correct. Each step follows from the one before, so you can check it yourself, without trusting anyone and without relying on luck.

Sign off on a tool's conclusion only once you can trace it back step by step.

Hold the needle yourself

Ricci explained aloud and Xu wrote every sentence, which meant understanding each one well enough to put it into Chinese. They went through three drafts. Following someone else's explanation is not the same as being able to write it.

Close the tool and redo its derivation from scratch. Wherever you get stuck is what you still have to learn.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: The tool's answers are quick and right, and you are less and less able to say how they were reached.

Ask first: If one condition changed, could I work this answer out again myself?

Where it goes wrong: Reading it as 'invent everything from scratch'. Xu did not invent geometry. He took someone else's proofs and wrote them into words he could explain. The point is being able to redo it, not refusing to use it.

Lines to keep

Don't settle for the embroidered ducks. Ask for the needle.

What you can derive, you don't have to take on trust.

Write it out yourself, and the needle is in your hand.
