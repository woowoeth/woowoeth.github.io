Sima Qian

Why he did not kill himself

The letter to Ren An

Letter to Ren An, c. 93 BC

The convention was clear and he broke it. Years later he wrote one man a letter explaining exactly why.

Sima QianThe letter to Ren An

What actually happened?

In 99 BC a general named Li Ling surrendered to the Xiongnu. Sima Qian, the court historian, spoke a few words in his defence, and the emperor had him imprisoned and then castrated. For a man of his rank the honourable course was to kill himself, and he did not take it. Years later, writing to Ren An, he gave the reason plainly: what he could not bear was to die with the work unexpressed and leave nothing behind. He had moved the question of staying alive off his honour and onto an unfinished thing.

Swap the question about face for a question about work

The first question has no answer and sinks the longer it is held. The second has one, and the answer points at something to do. That substitution is the whole move in the letter: not that the shame was resolved, but that it was taken off the switch controlling whether he lived.

After a public disgrace, do not ask whether you deserve to be here. Ask what you still owe.

The hardest sentence went to one person

The letter is addressed to Ren An, not to the world. For roughly twenty years nobody knew what he thought, and he explained himself to no one else. A thing you cannot say does not have to be said to everyone. Said properly once, it stops being held in.

You do not need an audience. One person, the whole story, start to finish, is enough.

He did not wait to stop hurting

The letter is explicit that the shame never left: every time he thinks of it, he writes, the sweat soaks through his robe. He finished the history carrying that, not after curing it. Waiting until it no longer hurts usually means never starting.

Shame does not leave the room first. The move is to work while it is still standing there.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you did something you find shameful, or you were humiliated in public, and you cannot imagine facing anyone.

Ask first: is there one thing unfinished that only you can finish? Have you told the whole of it to one person, start to finish?

Where it goes wrong: hearing it as endurance is a virtue — he endured because the book was not done, not for its own sake; and shame is not something you think your way out of, he never did.

Lines to keep

Every time I think of this shame, the sweat soaks my robe.

A death may be heavier than Mount Tai or lighter than a feather.

He knew what was expected. The book was not finished.
