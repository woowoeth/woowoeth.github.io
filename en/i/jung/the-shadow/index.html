What you push down comes back

It jumps out of other people

Jung on the shadow and projection

The kind of person you can't stand — why they can enrage you instantly. Jung said part of you is in there.

Carl JungIt jumps out of other people

What actually happened?

Jung called the part of yourself you deny and push down the shadow. To become an acceptable person, everyone suppresses something: ambition, laziness, temper, wanting to be seen. Those things don't disappear. They move somewhere you can't see, and then jump out of other people. So the kind of person you least tolerate usually carries the very thing you won't own. The harder it was pushed down, the faster you're enraged.

Disproportionate dislike is a signal

Not liking someone is ordinary. But if the reaction is out of proportion — the mention of them irritates you, you have to comment even when it doesn't concern you — it is probably not only about them. Ordinary dislike doesn't consume this much.

People who loathe attention-seekers are usually the ones who most want to be seen.

Suppressed things don't vanish, they reroute

Jung's framing is conservation-like: what isn't acknowledged finds another exit. It can become fault-finding in others, a sudden loss of control, or a physical complaint. Acknowledging it is the least-effort option available.

The person who never gets angry, when they finally do, frightens everyone including themselves.

Owning it isn't acting on it

Admitting you'd also like to slack off, be praised, have the ambition, is not the same as slacking or grabbing. Jung means putting it back into consciousness where it can be managed — what's hidden can't be, what's on the table can.

People who can say 'I want that position too' are less likely to do something ugly to get it.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: there's a type of person you can't think about without anger, and you know your reaction is too big.

Ask first: which exact quality am I reacting to? Write it down, then ask whether I have it and never let it out.

Where it goes wrong: using it to excuse every dislike — some people should be disliked; and owning something isn't permission to do it. You own it in order to manage it.

Lines to keep

What you push down doesn't vanish. It changes exit.
