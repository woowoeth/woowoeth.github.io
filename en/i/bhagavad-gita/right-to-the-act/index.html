The Bhagavad Gita

A right to the action, never to the fruit

Chapter two, verse forty-seven

Bhagavad Gita 2.47

Two thousand years of quotation have made it sound like detachment. In place it is a boundary line drawn under pressure.

The Bhagavad GitaChapter two, verse forty-seven

What actually happened?

The verse everyone knows is 2.47: your right is to action alone, never to its fruits; let not the fruit be your motive, nor let attachment to inaction take hold. Gandhi called the book his spiritual dictionary. What the poster version loses is where the sentence is spoken. Not in a study, and not to a calm man. On a battlefield, to someone who has already dropped his bow and is about to pay for whatever he does next.

Draw the line: the act is yours, the yield is not

This is a division of property, not a consolation. Whether you do it, how, and how much today sit on your side. Whether the market takes it, the committee approves it, the other person replies, all sit on the far side. Most effort goes into rearranging the far side.

Put the thing in two columns, mine and not mine. Work only the left one.

Not asking for the harvest is not refusing to look at it

Results still get read, because they are information about where the work was wrong. The difference is what you do afterwards. Using them to adjust the next action is the point. Using them to decide whether to continue hands the wheel to something you do not steer.

Read the numbers for one purpose only: which part of tomorrow's action changes.

What the line saves you is the arithmetic of worth

People who are forever computing whether a thing is worth it spend most of their strength computing, and the finer the calculation the less they move. Once the boundary is drawn that strength goes back into the work, not because the outcome stopped mattering but because it was never calculable.

When you catch yourself pricing the whole venture again, go back to the one concrete thing due today.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you have pushed on something for a long time with nothing coming back.

Ask first: which parts of this are actually yours? When you last read the result, were you adjusting the work or deciding whether to keep going?

Where it goes wrong: hearing it as the outcome does not matter — it says do not let the outcome decide today; the work itself still has to improve.

Lines to keep

Your right is to action alone, never to its fruits.

Let not the fruit of action be your motive.

The finer the calculation, the less anyone moves.
