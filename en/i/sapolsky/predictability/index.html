Uncertainty hurts more than intensity

Predictability and control

Thirty years of baboon field research; stress physiology experiments

Same pressure, and one person breaks while another doesn't. What the actual variable turns out to be.

Robert SapolskyPredictability and control

What actually happened?

Sapolsky spent thirty years watching wild baboons in East Africa. Low-ranking animals ran chronically high stress hormones and worse health across the board. But rank wasn't the key thing: at the same low rank, individuals who could anticipate when they'd be harassed, and who had social allies, had markedly better markers. The lab agrees. At the same pressure, predictability and a sense of control cut the damage sharply. That moves the intervention from reducing stress to adding certainty and autonomy — usually far cheaper.

Definite bad news beats being left hanging

People and animals adapt to a certain bad. They don't adapt to uncertainty. Which is why delaying an announcement often does more harm than the announcement — for the whole delay, everyone is on call.

A restructuring where the plan never lands costs more than the plan itself. A firm date saves half of it.

Control is a cheap buffer

At identical workload, people who decide their own order and pace show a markedly lower stress response. Adding autonomy doesn't require removing any tasks and cuts the wear substantially.

Swap 'these five, in this order, today' for 'these five today, you choose the order'. Same load, different experience.

Social support is a physiological variable

Baboons with allies, and people with stable relationships, show better markers under equal pressure. This isn't reassurance, it's a measurable difference. Isolation is itself a health risk.

Keeping one or two colleagues you can be honest with through a brutal project beats an extra hour of sleep.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: under heavy pressure that isn't going to decrease.

Ask first: is there any way to make what's coming more predictable — a fixed date, a definite answer? Can I take back some say over the order and pace?

Where it goes wrong: turning 'control helps' into needing to control everything, which is a new load of its own.
