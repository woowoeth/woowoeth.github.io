The stop-doing list

Duan Yongping (Contemporary · 1961–) — The stop-doing list.

Starting and buildingModernThe stop-doing list
Founder of BBK, and the source of the culture behind OPPO and vivo, two of the largest phone brands on earth, neither of which invented anything. He is the Chinese investor who paid over six hundred thousand dollars for lunch with Warren Buffett. His two rules are subtractive: do the right thing before doing the thing right, and the moment you find something is wrong, stop, whatever it costs.

What actually happened?

In 2006 he won the auction for a charity lunch with Warren Buffett, bidding six hundred and twenty thousand dollars. What he says he took away was not a stock. It was a subtraction: an account of what a person will simply not do, held to long enough that it stops having to be argued each time. He has said as much about his own record — that it went well not because he knew what to do, but because he knew a great many things not to do.

Do the right thing, then do the thing right

The order is the whole point. Doing a thing right is a question of skill and can be learned slowly. Doing the right thing is a question of choice, and once that choice is wrong, more effort makes the result worse. Direction is the sign; execution is only the magnitude.

A team polishing the wrong product to a superb finish has built a motorway to the wrong destination. The better the road, the larger the loss.

The moment you find it is wrong, stop

Whatever it costs to stop today is the smallest cost available, because today's cost is fixed and known while the cost of continuing compounds and is not. The severity of the rule is that it deletes the most comfortable option in business, which is to watch it another quarter.

Every quarter a wrong direction survives pulls in another cohort, another budget and a few more public promises. The cut in three months is twice as thick.

Dare to be last

When a category first appears nobody knows whether the demand is real or where the holes are, and the pioneers answer with their own money. Enter once the answers are visible and build it better. The condition is brutal: on entry day your version has to be visibly better, or late is only late.

Music players, smartphones, electric cars — in each category the eventual winner was not the inventor.

A stop-doing list is more reliable than a to-do list

What you should do is uncertain and keeps moving. What you should not do follows from your principles and holds for years. The list also saves judgement: when something on it arrives there is nothing to argue about and no meeting to hold.

Write your three no matter how profitable, never rules. Half your future opportunity reviews will end in ten seconds.

How do I use it today?

This week find one thing you know is wrong and are still doing, and stop it. You do not need a replacement lined up first. Stopping the wrong thing is a separate action from finding the right one, and it is the one that gets postponed.
