Subtraction is harder than addition

Platform Sutra, chapter one

Two verses. Everyone admired one; the other went to an illiterate kitchen worker. What separates them.

HuinengSubtraction is harder than addition

What actually happened?

The fifth patriarch asked his students for a verse to decide his successor. Shenxiu, the head monk and everyone's expected heir, wrote on the corridor wall at night: the body is a bodhi tree, the mind a bright mirror's stand; polish it constantly, let no dust settle. It is an entirely correct account of practice — wipe daily, keep the dust off. Huineng, illiterate and pounding rice out the back, had his written beside it: there was never a tree, nor any stand; there was never a thing there, so where would dust settle? He doesn't dispute the polishing. He asks where the mirror came from.

Shenxiu's verse isn't wrong. It's heavy

'Polish it constantly' is executable, checkable and almost impossible to get wrong. The trouble is it can never be finished: as long as you assume a mirror that gets dirty, wiping has no end. A great deal of effort is stuck in that correct, infinite loop.

Managing anxiety with more lists, more reviews, more reminders. The stack grows and the anxiety doesn't move — each addition is one more thing to maintain.

Ask who installed the premise

Huineng's move pushes the question back a layer: don't optimise the solution, audit the setup. 'There was never a thing there' means the object you keep wiping may be one you erected yourself. Remove the premise and the whole discipline goes with its anxiety.

'Everyone has to be satisfied with me' makes every conversation exhausting. Ask who set it. Usually you did, and it can be unset.

Subtraction is hard because it has no progress bar

Adding a method gives you something to do immediately. Removing a premise gives you nothing to do at all. The second pays far better and shows no progress along the way, so most people keep wiping.

Cutting half the meetings beats adding a collaboration tool. The first produces nothing to report, so almost nobody does it.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: something you're managing carefully has never once got lighter.

Ask first: does this need constant managing because it's inherently like that, or because I set a premise?

Where it goes wrong: reading subtraction as giving up; optimising only at the level of solutions and never inspecting the setup.
