Reform and the reckoning

Zhang Juzheng (Ming · 1525–1582) — Reform and the reckoning.

Power and organisationEarly modernReform and the reckoning
In two hundred and seventy years of Ming rule he is the one chief minister who actually changed the state. The single whip reform merged the tax system, the evaluation ledgers put every order on a name and a date, and land was resurveyed; the treasury went from empty to four million taels of silver in reserve. He was the most powerful man alive while he lived, and within a year of his death his house was raided and his family destroyed. This is the fullest version of the reformer's fate in the collection.

What actually happened?

His power rested on a triangle: a boy emperor who was the formal authority, the empress dowager who was the real one, and the chief eunuch who controlled the palace. He secured the last two, and only then could he govern. With the boy he was severe — diligence, frugality, lessons, a childhood entirely managed, from the age of ten. He died at the height of his power. The emperor, now grown, took his revenge within the year: the estate seized, the eldest son driven to suicide, the reforms picked apart. It was politics, and it was also ten years of a boy waiting.

The triangle was not an institution

His authority came from three personal relationships, not from any power the office of chief minister actually held. When the dowager lost influence and the eunuch was purged, the base vanished at once. Personal power is fragile; only institutional power lasts.

Ask what happens to your position if the two people who back you leave. If the answer is nothing survives, you have influence, not a role.

A reform needs beneficiaries who can speak

The single whip helped ordinary taxpayers, who had no political voice at all. The officials and local gentry it hurt had plenty. So the moment the protection was gone, one side could act and the other could not.

Design the beneficiaries deliberately, and check that they are people with the standing to defend what they got.

Do not suppress the person who will outlive you

Ten years of strict tutoring produced an emperor who was competent and who hated him. In power relationships the benefactor and the oppressor are often the same person, and which one you turn out to have been is decided by the other party, later.

The strong mentor makes the junior. The first thing the junior does once made is get out from under the mentor.

Assessment is the dangerous reform

The evaluation ledgers made government work. They also created, one by one, every official who later had a reason to want him gone. The cost of a reform is not the resistance in front of you; it is the enemies waiting behind it.

Everyone supports fairness before performance review arrives. Afterwards, the people it marked down start looking for a way back.

How do I use it today?

If you are pushing a change through an organisation, spend as much time designing who benefits as designing the change. Make the gains land on people with enough standing to defend them, and make sure they know the gains came from the new arrangement. A reform with no constituency is undone the week its sponsor leaves.
