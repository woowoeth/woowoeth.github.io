Eighty days, then poor for life

Tao Yuanming (365–427 · Xunyang) — Eighty days, then poor for life.

Body and daily lifeQin, Han and the Six DynastiesEighty days, then poor for life
A poet, and the founder of the Chinese poetry of retreat. At forty-one he took the magistracy of Pengze and held it a little over eighty days. An inspector came down from the commandery and his staff told him to dress properly and go out to receive the man; he said he could not bend his back for five pecks of rice to a petty official of the district, and unfastened the seal that same day. He never held office again. The poverty was real: at forty-four a fire took the house, and one year the grain ran out and he went out to beg and wrote a poem about it. He refused every summons the court afterwards sent.

What actually happened?

He wrote a short self-portrait in the third person and gave the man in it no name, only five willows by the house. Nobody knows where he came from. He does not chase reputation. He likes reading and does not labour over fine points, and when something in a book strikes him he forgets to eat. He likes wine and is too poor to buy it, so friends have him over, and when he drinks he means to get drunk and then leaves without ceremony. The walls are bare, the clothes patched, the food basket often empty, and he is at ease. He writes to please himself.

Turning is cheap only while you have not gone far

What the resignation poem presses is not resolve but distance: the road was wrong and he has not gone far enough to be unable to return. The price of turning rises with time, and eighty days back is not the price of eight years back. What breaks it is usually something small.

Turning the moment you notice is cheapest. Turning after a full term means you already owe the term.

Take the price of the road you chose, without blame or polish

He writes himself as neither victim nor saint. He keeps accounts: hungry, went out to beg, could not get the words out at the door. Twenty-odd years poor and he never went back, both true at once, which is what shows the turn was priced rather than impulsive.

The test of a wrong turn is whether you want to go back, not whether it hurts.

The no is answerable when the yes is not

By mistake I fell into the dusty net, and thirty years went by. Note the wording: not I always wanted to farm, but I was never suited to that. Most people are stopped by what do you want, which demands a positive answer. Cross off what you definitely will not have and the field narrows by itself.

If you cannot list what you want, list three things you absolutely will not have.

How do I use it today?

If you know this is not it and keep waiting for a better moment, work out how far from far you are, and what the small daily irritation is actually showing you. If you cannot say what you want, write down the three things you will definitely not have, and then set a next step small enough that you will certainly hit it.

One is about living well inside an arrangement, the other about making the decision. Pu SonglingBoth were poor for life: Pu Songling kept sitting the exam and never passed, Tao Yuanming stopped sitting it on purpose. One had no choice, the other chose. Du FuOne walked, one had no choice: Tao Yuanming knew where he was going the day he resigned, while Du Fu left office and searched the whole way southwest before stopping at Chengdu.

Lines to keep

He left at forty-one and never went back in twenty-odd years.

Own the price of the road and there is nothing to resent.

Cross off the no first and the road narrows by itself.
