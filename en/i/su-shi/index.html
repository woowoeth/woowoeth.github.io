Su Shi (Northern Song · 1037–1101) — Unbowed.

Mind and feelingMedievalUnbowed
One of the great Chinese poets, and also a calligrapher, a painter, a hydraulic engineer, a cook and a provincial governor. He was banished three times, each further than the last, and got steadily more open-hearted as it happened — not enduring the worst circumstances of his life but finding the best of himself inside them. Almost everything he is remembered for was written after his career ended.

What actually happened?

It is worth knowing the range before the philosophy. The same man who wrote the most quoted lines in the language dredged the West Lake at Hangzhou and built a causeway across it that still carries his name; set up what may be China's first public clinic; campaigned to stop infant drowning in Huangzhou; lived on the city wall during a flood at Xuzhou saying that while he was there the water would not take the city; and left recipes. None of that is decoration on the poetry. It is the same disposition doing different work.

The openness has a structure

It wasn't a temperament he was born with. His writing records the sequence: the blow lands, there is a long stretch of real pain, attention turns inward, the present moment turns out to be full, and only then the release. He didn't skip to serenity. He walked through the middle.

Two people laid off the same week. One posts about the fresh start on day three and is still hollow in month six; the other is wretched for a month, then starts.

The constraint produced the work

His three years in Huangzhou produced the two Red Cliff rhapsodies and his best-known lyrics. Having lost the ability to change anything outside himself, he was left with what he could still do — and that turned out to be the most concentrated output of his life.

The book got written the year the funding fell through, because there was nothing left to run and the writing was the only thing still in his hands.

He lost the politics and won everything else

He opposed the reformers' new laws, then opposed the conservatives when they abolished all of them. He held to the measure rather than the side, and both sides exiled him for it. In the political record he is a failure. A thousand years later he is the one people quote.

The one who wouldn't sign either faction's memo got passed over twice, and is the person both sides quote when they explain what went wrong.

How do I use it today?

Next time you're overruled, shelved, or watching something fail, try treating the stretch that follows as an enforced turn inward. The best thinking and the best work often happen in the period when there is nothing to administer.

Read alongside

ZhuangziTwo ways of not being owned: Zhuangzi refuses the post, Su Shi takes it and refuses to be defined by losing it. C.S. LewisBoth wrote through the worst of it. Lewis to find out what he actually believed, Su Shi to find out what was still enjoyable. Chu ShijianBoth began with one small concrete job after losing the position: Su Shi broke ground at Huangzhou, Chu Shijian built the irrigation first. Neither waited to be recognised again. SenecaTwo ways of writing out of adversity: Seneca writes about restraint from inside the machinery of power, Su Shi writes about lightness on the road into exile, and both had been tested before either wrote a line. Viktor FranklCreation under punishment: Su Shi wrote his best work in exile, Frankl finished logotherapy in the camps — both took the worst stretch of a life and made it the productive one. Du FuBoth rebuilt after forty: Su Shi broke ground at Huangzhou, Du Fu put up a roof at Chengdu, and both took what a life runs on before any position. Su Shi went back; Du Fu never did. Tao YuanmingTwo ways out of office: Su Shi was exiled and moved by other people, Tao Yuanming walked out himself on the day. One is about living well inside an arrangement, the other about making the decision.
