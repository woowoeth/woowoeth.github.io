What actually happened?

Put the last two pieces together and you get a method that runs against common sense: to stop something, don't fight the thing itself first. Look at what keeps it alive. A habit that lasts usually has a whole set of props — a fixed hour, a fixed place, nothing else to hand, people doing it with you. Withdraw those one at a time and the thing gets harder by itself, whereas fighting it head-on withdraws only your willpower, which is the first thing to run out.

Keep the log before you move anything

Log ten in a row: the hour, the place, whether anything else was in your hands, who was there. Most people finish the log and find the trigger isn't craving, it's one recurring empty slot. Finding the slot is worth more than studying how to resist.

Eight of ten were in bed before sleep. What needs changing is that half hour.

Whatever replaces it has to actually work

Removing something leaves a gap, and a gap gets filled back the same way. What worked in Rat Park wasn't the absence of morphine, it was companions, wheels, somewhere to go. So the second step is putting something you genuinely like into that slot, not leaving it bare.

Replacing the phone with staring at the ceiling rarely holds. Replacing it with something your hands are doing does.

When the cage can't be moved, this doesn't apply

The method assumes the cage is movable. Some situations aren't — you can't leave the job, move the family, replace the person. Forcing 'change your environment' there only adds a layer of failure. That's when to find professional help rather than keep pushing on the situation.

Blaming yourself for not changing an environment you genuinely cannot change is effort in the wrong place.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you want to stop something, you've tried, each attempt ran on holding out, and each broke in the same place.

Ask first: for the last ten times — what hour, what place, what state? Find the common factor and move that, not the thing itself.

Where it goes wrong: turning it into a universal explanation that replaces medical help; and don't assume removal is the end. The vacated slot needs filling or it fills itself back.

Lines to keep

Take apart the props holding it up, not the thing itself.

The trigger is often not craving. It's one recurring empty slot.

The slot you vacate will be filled back the same way.
