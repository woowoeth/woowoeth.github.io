When anxious, move before you think

Start with the physiology

Spark, the chapters on anxiety and stress

The more you think, the worse it gets, and knowing that doesn't stop it. Why this is the moment to go outside.

John RateyStart with the physiology

What actually happened?

Ratey points out how physical anxiety is: the body has already gone on standby and the hormones are circulating, and talking yourself down at the cognitive level barely reaches it. Exercise works because it acts on that system directly — burning off the standby hormones and giving the brain the signal that you already ran and the danger has passed. That explains a common experience: after a walk the thing is less frightening, and nothing about it has changed. So his advice is blunt: when the feeling is jammed, handle the physiology first, the thinking second.

Bring the arousal down before you reason

Reasoning at high arousal is extremely inefficient, with yourself or with anyone else. The order should be: get the body down — walk, breathe, leave the room — then return to the content. It's also why reasoning mid-argument doesn't work.

Chest tight and the same thought circling: go outside for twenty minutes. Come back to the same thing and it weighs differently.

Rumination has to be interrupted, not resolved

The twentieth pass over the same thought produces no new information, only a deeper groove. You don't get out of that loop by continuing to think. It needs an external action to break it, and exercise is the most reliable — it occupies attention and burns the hormones at once.

Catching yourself chewing the same sentence again is the signal to interrupt, not the signal to keep going.

Don't let it replace the problem

Exercise lowers the intensity of anxiety. It doesn't resolve what caused it. What you buy is a state in which you can think properly, not an escape. The thing is still there when you get back — you can just see it more clearly.

Running daily to hold down a fear about a decision. However far you run, the decision is still waiting.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: something has gone round your head many times and each pass makes it worse.

Ask first: am I thinking or ruminating? Move for twenty minutes, then come back to the same thing.

Where it goes wrong: using exercise to avoid, so that once you've moved you never deal with the thing at all.

Lines to keep
