The Yellow Emperor's Inner Classic

Anger sends it up, brooding ties it in a knot

A hundred ills come from qi: feeling does not stay in the heart, it travels the body

Basic Questions, 'On Pain', opening

After a row at home my chest is tight for days. What does a feeling leave behind in the body once it has passed?

What actually happened?

The Emperor opens this chapter by setting a rule. One who speaks well of heaven must show it in people, one who speaks well of the past must show it fits today, one who speaks well of people must find it holds in himself. Then he says what he has concluded: a hundred ills come from qi. Anger sends it up, joy slackens it, grief wears it away, fear sinks it, shock scatters it, and brooding ties it in a knot.

It has to hold in you

The Emperor states the test before the conclusion: one who speaks well of people must find it holds in himself. A line about people counts only if you can check it against yourself. Anger sends qi against its course, and in the worst case, the text says, blood is vomited.

Reading 'anger sends it up', you recall your neck and shoulders stiff for days after the last row.

Of nine kinds, even joy is one

Anger, grief and fear are on the list, but so are joy, cold, heat and toil. Of joy it says the qi eases and the spirit reaches its goal: good news, yet still a shift. Nine kinds go nine different ways; it is not only the bad feelings that cost you.

A whole week of busy happy events, and you still collapse on Saturday.

Brooding ties it in a knot

When the heart holds a thing, the spirit settles on it, and right qi lingers and does not move, so the qi is knotted. The one closest to daily life: not rage, not tears, but one sentence turned over again and again.

You replay what was said in the afternoon thirty times at night; next morning your chest is still tight.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: a row at home is over, but you are not: chest tight, no appetite, awake at three.

Ask first: where in my body does that sentence still sit? Name which of the nine it is, anger, grief or brooding, before telling yourself to stop thinking about it.

Where it goes wrong: treating it as a diagnosis and writing yourself a prescription: 'my qi is knotted, so...'. The text says feeling and body are in traffic, not what to take for it. If you have real symptoms, see a doctor.

Lines to keep

Joy is among the nine, and it costs too.
