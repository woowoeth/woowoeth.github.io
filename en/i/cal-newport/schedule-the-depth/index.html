The gap will not appear on its own

Deep Work, part three

The thing you will do when you have time never starts. How to take the time instead.

Cal NewportThe gap will not appear on its own

What actually happened?

Newport's central operational advice is to schedule deep work the way you schedule a meeting: in the diary, announced to other people, entered when the time comes, rather than waited for. The reason is blunt. A gap never appears by itself, and shallow work expands like a gas to fill whatever space is available. He also argues for capping shallow work and handling it in batches, so what remains can join up into blocks. None of it relies on willpower: holding attention by self-control in a place where anyone can interrupt you is a design failure.

Taking the time works better than managing the will

Self-control is consumable; a diary is not. Fix the deep block the way a meeting is fixed and you no longer have to make the decision about whether to concentrate today. Making that decision is itself what wears people out.

Marking two hours each morning as unbookable is more reliable than asking yourself every morning when you will write.

Cap the shallow work; do not try to abolish it

Mail, coordination and meetings are necessary and they build no new capability. Newport does not propose removing them. He proposes a ceiling and a fixed slot: the total is unchanged, and the block survives.

Handle mail at ten in the morning and four in the afternoon rather than whenever it lands.

Boredom is trained

If every gap gets filled with a phone, the brain grows used to a constant supply of novelty, and anything demanding a long sit afterwards becomes unusually painful. So part of training attention is letting yourself be bored.

Two minutes waiting for the lift with the phone in your pocket. Small moments like that decide whether you can sit an hour.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: the important and non-urgent thing never gets in.

Ask first: does it have a slot in my diary? If not, what exactly is the gap I am waiting for going to arrive from?

Where it goes wrong: filling the diary with deep blocks and executing none of them. Hold one, do it, then add.

Lines to keep

A gap never appears by itself. You have to schedule it.

Fill every gap with stimulation and you lose the ability to concentrate.

You have a finite amount of willpower that becomes depleted as you use it.
