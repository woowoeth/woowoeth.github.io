What actually happened?

When war broke out, wounded men had to be carried far behind the lines before they could be X-rayed, and many didn't survive the journey. She left the laboratory, raised money, had vehicles converted with generators and X-ray equipment, learned to drive and to repair the cars herself, and took them near the front with her teenage daughter Irène. The soldiers called them the little Curies. She published almost nothing during those years.

Point the craft at what's needed here

Her field was research, and what was scarce then was not a new discovery but getting a wounded man photographed today. A craft is only settled inside a specific situation, and situations change. What your field is matters less than what's missing now.

Ask: what specific thing in front of me needs this skill most right now?

She didn't wait for a role worthy of her. She learned to drive

With her standing she could have lent her name and nothing else. What she learned instead was driving and vehicle repair — work with no status in it. Getting anything actually done always contains a stretch of unglamorous work nobody gets to skip.

To get something done, take on the least prestigious part of it first.

Years with no output aren't wasted years

Academically those years are nearly blank. Counted in papers, they were squandered. Counted in people who lived, they weren't. You have to decide yourself which ledger your life settles in, or you will live by somebody else's standard.

Before judging a stretch of time, decide what the ledger is: output, usefulness, or something else.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you have real skills and what's in front of you can't use them, or the work that could looks beneath you.

Ask first: what specific thing most needs this skill now? Will I take the least prestigious part of it? What ledger am I settling these years in?

Where it goes wrong: hearing it as 'your field doesn't matter' — what she did was directly related to her expertise, just used differently; and setting it down was temporary. She went back to the laboratory after the war.
