She took over his course

Starting where he stopped

Pierre Curie died April 1906; her first Sorbonne lecture, 5 November 1906

The person who mattered most is suddenly gone and the days still have to continue. What she did seven months later.

Marie CurieStarting where he stopped

What actually happened?

On 19 April 1906 Pierre Curie was knocked down by a horse-drawn wagon in a Paris street and killed instantly. The Sorbonne gave his chair to his widow. On 5 November she gave her first lecture, with the room overflowing and journalists present, everyone waiting to hear her say something about him. She offered no eulogy. She opened at the point where her husband's last lecture had stopped, and continued. She became the first woman to hold a professorship there.

Catch hold of one specific thing that must be done

An enormous blank can't be faced directly. What can be faced is something with a time, a place and a content: two o'clock, that room, that course. It doesn't solve the grief. It gives the day a shape you can put yourself inside.

For the next few days, fix one thing that has to be done and has an hour and a place attached.

Continuing looks more like keeping faith than a farewell

Everyone was waiting for a leave-taking; she carried on. She didn't turn the lecture into a ceremony, she finished the passage he hadn't finished. The most concrete way to commemorate someone is often to keep doing the thing they had in hand.

If you want to do something for someone who has gone, look for the unfinished thing you could take on.

When people are waiting for a performance, you may just work

The crowd had come to see a widow's grief. She didn't provide it. Nobody in the worst of it is obliged to bring their pain out for viewing; finishing the work in front of you is a complete account of yourself.

If you don't want to speak, don't. Finish what's in your hands. You owe nobody a performance.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: the most important person has just gone, life has to continue, and you don't know which step is first.

Ask first: is there one thing in the next few days that has to be done, at a set hour in a set place? Is there something of theirs I could take on? Am I forcing myself to give someone else an account?

Where it goes wrong: hearing 'keep busy and it stops hurting' — in the same period she was writing a grief journal, lecturing by day and speaking to him at night. Work is not a way round mourning.
