Harmony is not sameness

Agreement and consent are different things

Analects 13.23

Nobody objected in the meeting. That is usually not agreement, and the distinction he draws says what it is instead.

The AnalectsAgreement and consent are different things

What actually happened?

The gentleman harmonises without conforming; the small man conforms without harmony. Harmony is different things holding together; conformity is flattening the difference until only one thing is left. He offers this as a way of telling two kinds of person apart rather than two kinds of atmosphere, and the reason is practical: whether difference survives in a room depends on whether people feel safe producing it, and that is settled by whoever holds the power in the room.

A unanimous room is a bad signal

When a group agrees completely about something complicated, there are two possibilities. Either it does not matter, or somebody is not talking. It is usually the second. The disagreement has not gone anywhere; it has moved outside the meeting.

No questions at the review. After launch the problems arrive one by one, and every one of them had been seen by somebody beforehand.

Someone has to pay for the first no

The first person to object carries the whole risk, and everyone who agrees afterwards carries none. So silence is not the absence of views, it is the price of going first. For harmony to happen, whoever holds the power has to buy that first no themselves.

Name the worst weakness in your own proposal, or ask the most senior person in the room to argue against it. Both beat telling people to speak freely.

Difference does not have to be resolved

Harmony means the disagreement can stay where it is while the work goes forward. Plenty of arguments cannot be settled until something has been built and looked at. Forcing agreement before anyone moves pushes the disagreement underground.

Two camps, two directions. Rather than argue until one surrenders, agree on a number and a date that would settle it. The data answers faster than the room.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: an important decision, and not one person in the room raised an objection.

Ask first: if someone did disagree, what would it cost him to say so here?

Where it goes wrong: reading no objection as consent; or demanding that everyone be convinced before anything moves, which buries the disagreement rather than settling it.

Lines to keep

The gentleman is inclusive and not partisan; the small man the reverse.
