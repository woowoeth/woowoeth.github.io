Goujian of Yue

Starting over

Goujian of Yue (Spring and Autumn · Yue · took the throne 496 BC) — Starting over.

Strategy and competitionPre-Qin and classicalStarting over
Goujian became king of Yue in 496 BC. Three years later he attacked Wu against Fan Li's advice, was crushed, and was besieged on Mount Kuaiji with five thousand men. He survived by becoming the vassal of King Fuchai of Wu. Over the next twenty-odd years he rebuilt, destroyed Wu, and became the last hegemon of the Spring and Autumn period. Everything here follows Sima Qian. He is here for two situations: what to let go of when you start over from a fall, and when to move once you are ready.

What actually happened?

After the defeat, Goujian retreated with five thousand men to Mount Kuaiji, and Wu surrounded them. He sent his minister Wen Zhong to the Wu camp to crawl forward on his knees and offer Goujian as Wu's servant and his wife as a concubine. Wu's adviser Wu Zixu said refuse. Goujian wanted to kill his own family, burn his treasures and fight to the death. Wen Zhong stopped him and bribed Wu's chief minister instead, and Wu's king pardoned Yue. Trapped, Goujian sighed: is this where I end? Wen Zhong listed founders who had been imprisoned or exiled, and all of them later ruled.

When you did not listen, say so first

In 494 BC he heard Wu was drilling troops day and night and wanted to strike first. Fan Li told him not to. I have decided, he said. After the rout his first words to Fan Li were: this is because I did not listen to you; what now?

When you push something through and it fails, go back to the person who warned you and say so before asking what next.

Give the job to whoever fits it

Back home he wanted Fan Li to run the state. Fan Li said: in war, Wen Zhong is not my equal; in calming the country and winning the people, I am not his. Goujian gave the government to Wen Zhong, and Fan Li went to Wu as a hostage for two years.

In the years of rebuilding, give the key posts to the people who fit them best, not the people closest to you.

After twenty-two years, the hard part is not relenting

At the end Wu's king, besieged on a hill, sent an envoy stripped to the waist to beg for peace, recalling that he had once spared Goujian. Goujian could not bear it and wanted to agree. Fan Li asked whether twenty-two years of planning should be thrown away in one morning, and drummed the advance.

When the rival you spent years beating comes to plead, first remember why you needed to win.

How do I use it today?

This week write your most recent fall as one sentence: where you lost, who warned you, and which warning you ignored. Put a copy somewhere you open every day, and for a month, each time you see it, ask whether today's work has anything to do with that sentence.

Xiang YuBoth lost a decisive war down to almost nothing. Goujian, trapped at Kuaiji, bowed as a vassal, went home to farm, and twenty-odd years later destroyed Wu. Xiang Yu, at the Wu River, was urged to cross home and start again; eight thousand men had crossed with him and none returned, he said, and he could not face their elders. One dropped his dignity and started over. The other kept it and did not.
