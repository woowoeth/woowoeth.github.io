The cook and the ox

Go along the gaps

Zhuangzi, The Secret of Caring for Life

One cook replaces his blade monthly, another hasn't in nineteen years. Where exactly the difference sits.

ZhuangziGo along the gaps

What actually happened?

Cook Ding carves an ox for Lord Wenhui and the movement looks like dancing. Asked how his technique got so good, he says what he cares about isn't technique. At first he saw a whole ox; after three years he no longer saw a whole ox; now he meets it with the spirit rather than looking with the eye. A good cook changes his blade yearly, because he cuts. An ordinary one monthly, because he hacks. His has done thousands of oxen in nineteen years and the edge is as if newly whetted — because he has been travelling in the gaps between the joints.

The blade stays sharp because it never meets the hard part

The edge has no thickness and the joints have space; something thin entering something wide moves easily. Struggle isn't a shortage of force. It's the wrong place to put the blade. Every hard collision is a positioning problem.

A push that meets resistance everywhere usually means the entry point sits where the interests are densest. Enter where nobody objects and it moves itself.

See the structure before you talk about technique

Ding's three stages — seeing a whole ox, ceasing to see a whole ox, meeting it with the spirit — are increasingly fine readings of structure. Technique is a by-product of that reading. Train it in the other order and you are training force.

A new hire handles a complaint with scripts. A veteran hears which step it's actually stuck at. The difference is the map in their head.

Slow down where it knots

Ding isn't smooth throughout: at every knot he sees the difficulty, grows wary, slows, and makes his movements smaller. Real fluency isn't speed the whole way. It's knowing where you must go slowly.

For the two steps most likely to go wrong, add a check instead of adding speed. People with one tempo have all their accidents in one place.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: something is hard to push at every point, and you're considering pushing harder.

Ask first: is my blade in a gap or on a bone?

Where it goes wrong: substituting force for position; holding one tempo throughout and not slowing where you most need to.
