Zhang Qian

Going first

Zhang Qian (Western Han · d. 114 BCE) — Going first.

Starting and buildingQin, Han and the Six DynastiesGoing first
Western Han envoy who went west twice, with a biography in Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian. Sent by Emperor Wu to recruit the Yuezhi against the Xiongnu, he was held captive for over ten years, escaped, found the Yuezhi unwilling to fight, and came home with an account of a dozen kingdoms nobody in Han had seen. He is here for 'Do I jump in now', where nothing yet says what is left when you go in first and the original goal fails.

What actually happened?

Arriving late to a battle against the Xiongnu, Zhang Qian was sentenced to death, paid a ransom, and lost his title. He then proposed to the emperor that the Wusun in the far west could be won over. He was sent again with three hundred men, two horses each, herds in the tens of thousands and a fortune in gold and silk. The Wusun would not move east, so he sent his deputies on to other kingdoms. He died about a year after returning. A year later the deputies began arriving with envoys from those kingdoms.

Others travelled under his name

After his death, Han envoys going west all called themselves by his title, the Marquis of Bowang, to win trust abroad, and it worked. The first one through leaves a road and also a credit others can borrow.

In a new industry, the first firm known for keeping its word lets later firms skip rounds of negotiation by citing it.

A road inferred from a walking stick

In Bactria he saw bamboo canes and cloth from Shu, in the southwest of Han, and was told merchants bought them in India. He reasoned that India could not be far from Shu. The emperor sent men to find that road; hill peoples blocked them and it was never opened.

On a shelf in a foreign market, notice the thing that should not be there, and ask how it arrived.

After the disgrace, his knowledge was the asset

Stripped of his title, he did not recover by begging. He recovered because he knew better than anyone which kingdoms lay west and how each stood with the Xiongnu. What the failed first trip gathered paid for the second.

When the project dies and the role goes with it, what you learned on the front line is often what you still own.

How do I use it today?

Today, write three lines about the new thing you are working on: the original goal; if it fails, what this trip can still bring back; and where you are recording what you see.
