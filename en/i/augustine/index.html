Grant me chastity, but not yet

Augustine (354–430 · Hippo, North Africa) — Grant me chastity, but not yet.

Mind and feelingQin, Han and the Six DynastiesGrant me chastity, but not yet
A bishop in Roman North Africa, and the author of the Confessions, which he wrote in his forties about the first half of his own life. It is the first book in the West that reports from inside the experience of knowing exactly what to do and not doing it. He is here not as a theologian but as a witness: he wanted to change, he could not, and he took the failure apart while it was still happening to him. Sixteen centuries before habit science, he had already located the problem in the will rather than in the knowledge.

What actually happened?

Book Eleven of the Confessions stops the autobiography dead and asks what time is. The answer is one of the most quoted sentences in philosophy: if nobody asks me, I know; if I want to explain it to someone who asks, I do not know. Then, instead of retreating behind the paradox, he keeps going, and works out that past and future have no existence outside a present mind holding them. It is the same procedure he uses on his own failures. Take an experience everybody has, refuse the ready-made account of it, and keep looking until the mechanism shows.

The wanting is real, and so is the not yet

Grant me chastity and continence, but not yet. He is clear that both halves were sincere: he wanted the outcome and he did not want today. So reinforcing the wanting does nothing, because that side was never short. Not yet is a permit that renews itself every morning, and it will not lapse on its own.

How many times have you said after this busy stretch? Somebody has to tear that up; it does not expire.

Some wrongs have no reason at all

At sixteen he and a gang stripped a neighbour's pear tree. There were better pears at home and they threw most of the stolen ones to the pigs. Decades later he hunts for a motive and concludes there was none, only an occasion: alone, he says, he would never have gone.

If why did I do that has produced nothing after twenty attempts, cross the question out and look at who was standing there.

Not a weak will, a divided one

In the garden he notices that the mind commands the body and is instantly obeyed, while the mind commands itself and is resisted. His conclusion is that there are two wills and both are his. That is why more resolve does not help: it adds to one side while the other stays exactly where it was.

I do not want to is not laziness. Some part of you is guarding something. Ask what it is guarding before you decide again.

How do I use it today?

Take the thing you keep not starting and count how many times this month you have said not yet, then name the one comfort that starting tonight would cost you. That comfort, not a shortage of resolve, is what the other half of you is protecting.
