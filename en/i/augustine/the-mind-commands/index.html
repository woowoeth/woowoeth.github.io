Augustine

The mind commands itself and is resisted

Not a weak will, two of them

Confessions, Book VIII, chapter 9

He notices something strange in the garden: the body obeys instantly and the will does not. His explanation is a mechanism, not a scolding.

AugustineNot a weak will, two of them

What actually happened?

Book Eight is an afternoon in a garden. He wants to change, cannot, and tears at his hair and beats his forehead. Then he sets the oddity down exactly: the mind commands the body and is instantly obeyed; the mind commands itself and is resisted. Why does the hand listen when he does not? The answer he reaches is that there are two wills, one old and one new, and both of them are his. Neither command ever gets more than half of him.

Not too little will, but a divided one

People who understand everything and still do not move are told to want it harder. He says the resolve is already there, and so is a resolve pointing the other way. Adding to one side leaves the other side exactly where it was.

One more decision usually does nothing, because the part that refuses is pulling on the same rope.

The half that refuses is also you

He does not call the reluctant will an enemy or a defect. He says both are himself. Treating the refusing half as an intruder to be crushed only tightens the deadlock. Granting that it is yours lets you ask what it is guarding.

I do not want to is not laziness. Some part of you is protecting something. Ask what.

It broke by resolving, not by force

He did not finally shove his way across on willpower. Something happened in that garden that brought the two halves into one. The event cannot be copied, but the direction can: look for the condition that ends the tug of war rather than for more strength.

When you are stuck, stop escalating. Go looking for what would let both halves want the same thing.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: you understand the argument completely, you have decided many times, and nothing moves.

Ask first: what is the half of you that refuses actually protecting? If that thing were met some other way, would the deadlock hold?

Where it goes wrong: concluding that will is useless and drifting — he did change, only not by force; and two wills is not an excuse, both of them are you.

Lines to keep

The mind commands the body and is instantly obeyed.

The mind commands itself and is resisted.

Two wills, one old and one new. Both are yours.
