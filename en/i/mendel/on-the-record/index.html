Print it and leave it

When nobody reads your work, make sure it can be found

Gregor Mendel, 'Experiments in Plant Hybridisation' (1866); William Bateson, Mendel's Principles of Heredity: A Defence (1902)

The work is solid and nobody pays attention. What should you do with it while nobody is looking?

Gregor MendelWhen nobody reads your work, make sure it can be found

What actually happened?

In February and March 1865 Mendel read his pea experiments to the Natural History Society of Brünn, and the next year the paper appeared in the society's proceedings. For more than thirty years almost nobody cited it. He died in 1884. In 1900 Hugo de Vries, Carl Correns and Erich von Tschermak, working separately, reached the same ratios, and each of them pointed back to that paper. William Bateson, reading it, called the account "excellent and complete".

Write it so others can check it

He recorded how many plants were used, how many seeds they bore, of which kinds and in what ratio, down to extreme cases such as one plant with 14 round and 15 angular seeds. Thirty-four years later others laid their own counts beside his, and they matched.

For the piece nobody read, add how you did it and what the numbers were, so someone could repeat it.

A lecture lasts one evening; print lasts

Reading the paper aloud reached one room of listeners. Printing it put it on paper and onto shelves. The three men of 1900 could find it because of the printed copy, not because anyone remembered those two evenings.

Put finished work somewhere public, dated and searchable, not only on your own laptop.

Neglect is often bad timing

Bateson offered a reason for the silence: around 1865 new methods of research had opened up, and the most original investigators preferred them to the tedious observations of hybridisers. He still admitted that the total neglect of such a discovery was not easy to account for.

When everyone is chasing another field, an empty audience is not proof that you did the work badly.

How do I use it today?

Where you are: You made something you believe is solid, you put it out, and nobody paid attention. You are starting to think it was wasted.

Ask first: Is it written up completely? If someone wanted to check it one day, could they? Is it somewhere it can be found?

Where it goes wrong: Reading it as 'good work is always discovered, so just wait'. Mendel did not live to see it. It was found because of the complete record and the printed copy, and both of those he made himself.

Lines to keep
