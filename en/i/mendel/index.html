Gregor Mendel (Austrian Empire · 1822–1884) — Heredity.

Learning and growthThe industrial ageHeredity
Augustinian friar at the abbey in Brünn (now Brno) and the founder of genetics. Between 1856 and 1863 he crossed peas in the monastery garden, and in 1866 published 'Experiments in Plant Hybridisation', the first account of heredity in counted ratios. The paper was almost ignored until three botanists found it independently in 1900. He is here for two situations: how to make the thing you do on the side more than a hobby, and what to do with good work that nobody reads.

What actually happened?

In 1850 Mendel sat the exam to become a certified schoolteacher and failed its last part, the oral. His abbot, Cyril Napp, sent him to the University of Vienna, where from 1851 to 1853 he studied physics and natural science. Back in Brünn he taught as a substitute teacher. In 1856 he took the exam again and again failed the oral. That same year he planted the first peas for his crosses in the monastery garden.

One pair at a time

He chose seven pairs of characters that anyone could tell apart: round or wrinkled seed, yellow or green seed leaves, the colour of the seed coat, inflated or constricted pods, green or yellow unripe pods, flowers along the stem or at its end, tall or short stems. He followed one pair at a time.

To judge whether something is improving, watch one measure at a time, not ten at once.

What hides comes back

In the first hybrid generation only one character of each pair showed. He called it dominant and the hidden one recessive. When the hybrids set seed, the hidden character returned, in about one in four. Hidden is not gone.

A pupil who shows no progress this term has not lost everything you taught.

After he became abbot

In 1868 he was elected abbot of the monastery. With the duties that came with it, his scientific work largely ended. Most of what he did in science was done in the dozen or so years before.

When the main job finally takes off, keep a fixed slot for the side thing you care about.

How do I use it today?

This week pick one thing you do on the side and care about, and choose a number to record every week: pieces written, sessions practised, people who came. Record it for four weeks before deciding whether it is working.

Read alongside

Charles DarwinThey lived at the same time and both asked how living things pass on what they are. Darwin gathered material for twenty years and wrote a book that covered everything and was argued over at once. Mendel worked on one plant, wrote one paper, and was not cited for thirty-four years. One went as wide as possible, the other as narrow; later genetics needed both.
