Starting and building

Chu Shijian

Up the mountain at seventy-four

Chu Shijian (1928–2019 · Yunnan) — Up the mountain at seventy-four.

Starting and buildingModernUp the mountain at seventy-four
A man from Yunnan who ran a cigarette factory from the age of fifty-one and made Hongtashan the best-selling brand in China, known for nailing down the standard at every step and tying every person's pay to quality. At seventy-four it was all gone: the position, the name, most of the savings, his health. He and his wife leased four hundred acres of bare hillside and planted sugar oranges, which take four or five years to bear. The fruit sold properly a decade later, when he was eighty-four.

What actually happened?

He was fifty-one when he was put in charge of a cigarette factory in Yuxi. What he did there was unglamorous and relentless: a written standard at every step of the process, and every person's pay tied to the quality of what came out rather than the quantity. Within a decade Hongtashan was the best-selling cigarette in the country. Two decades after that he was seventy-four, living in a work shed on a mountain with none of it left — no position, no name, no savings to speak of — except the way he ran things.

Too late is a subtraction problem

He never argued that he was still young. He counted: an orange tree fruits in four or five years, he was seventy-four, so the first crop landed at seventy-nine, an age most people reach. The numbers cleared, so he went. Too late is nearly always a feeling, and feelings cannot count years.

When something feels too late, write down the years it needs and the years you probably have, and look at the two numbers together.

The years with nothing to show are the years for foundations

While the trees carried nothing he built the irrigation, had every plot's soil tested, brought in agricultural researchers for the fertiliser mix, and cut the planting density because crowded trees give sour fruit. None of that is possible in a year with a harvest to bring in.

Spend the stretch before results arrive on the underlying work you will never get back to once you are busy.

The method travelled; the industry knowledge was hired

Tobacco and oranges have nothing in common, and he first touched an orange in his seventies. The management did not change: each plot contracted to one household, paid on quality rather than weight, pruning and fertiliser and picking all written down as rules. He never pretended to know oranges. He hired the people who did.

Changing field, sort what you know into two piles: what belongs to the industry and can be hired, and how you work, which travels with you.

A title is lent. A skill is grown

The first reaction to losing a position is that you are now nothing. The hillside proved the reverse: the title came from the seat and the skill came from the man, and only one of them could be taken back. Good times never make that distinction visible.

After the position goes, do not start with who am I. Start with what can I do. That list is longer than it feels.

How do I use it today?

Before deciding something is too late, put two numbers on paper: how many years the thing needs before it shows a result, and how many years you can reasonably expect to have. Only if the first fits inside the second does the question get interesting — and then ask what you are still holding as a route back.
